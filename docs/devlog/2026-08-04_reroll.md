# Devlog — reroll, minus the part that needed a snapshot

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Scope:** `/reroll` on turns that changed nothing

---

## The question that found it

Mid-session, after a narration failure printed a chain of thought into the story: *"I thought
we added an option to reroll a turn?"*

We had added `/retry`, which re-runs **extraction** over prose the player has already read. It
never re-narrates. So on that turn it would have been useless — the narration itself was the
garbage, and re-extracting a train of thought finds no world changes in it twice.

Real reroll was designed and shelved, blocked on one thing: deltas are not invertible.
`MoodChanged(hald, "wary")` does not record what the mood was before, so there is no undo to
compute, and rerolling needs a snapshot of canon taken before the turn was applied.

## The subset that needs no snapshot

**A turn that applied no deltas never moved canon, so there is nothing to undo.** Only the last
line of history has to go, and `ReplaceLastTurnAsync` already did that for `/retry`.

Measured against the 51-turn save: **12 of 51 turns applied nothing — 23%.**

And the coverage is better than that number suggests, because the failures worth rerolling are
concentrated in it. A narration failure produces no deltas *by definition* — garbage prose,
nothing extracted. So the free subset covers 100% of the case that prompted the question.

## What it does

`/reroll` re-narrates the last turn from the same player input, replaces the record in place,
and does not advance the turn counter. A turn that moved canon is refused, with the reason:

```
Cannot reroll: That turn changed canon (2 delta(s) applied), and deltas cannot be undone
without a snapshot of canon from before the turn.
```

A refusal is not an error and should not read like one. It is a limit with a known shape, and
stating it plainly is better than a command that half-works or fails obscurely.

**The discarded prose is never shown to the narrator.** The window is rebuilt from history with
the rerolled turn dropped — `LoadRecentAsync` grew a `skipLast` parameter for exactly this.
Without it the narrator sees the version being rejected and paraphrases it, which is the sort of
bug that never announces itself: the reroll works, the prose looks different enough to seem
resampled, and the feature is quietly worth much less than it appears. It has a self-test.

## Verified live

Same scene, rerolled:

> *first:* "The peat smoke hangs thick in the low-ceilinged room, stinging the eyes... Behind
> the counter, the innkeeper stands like a boulder in a faded apron."
>
> *rerolled:* "You take in the dim interior of the taproom... In the shadows of the corner, an
> old marsh-hand nurses a dented pewter mug."

Genuinely resampled rather than reworded — different framing, different details, the same
scene. That is temperature 0.9 doing its job, and it is why reroll can fix a bad turn while
`/retry` at temperature 0.0 mostly reproduces its own deltas. **The two commands do not do each
other's job**, and the help text now says so in one line each.

History after the reroll: one line, turn 1, the new prose. Canon at turn 1. Refusal verified
separately on a turn that applied two deltas.

## What generalises

- **A blocked feature is worth re-reading for the subset that is not blocked.** The snapshot
  requirement was recorded accurately and applied too broadly — it constrains turns that
  changed something, which is not all of them. Nothing new was learned to unblock this; the
  design already contained the answer.
- **The prompting question came from play, not from the plan.** This was not on the list for
  today. It surfaced because a real session hit a real failure and the obvious recovery was
  missing.

## Next

The other half — rerolling a turn that moved canon — still wants `canon.prev.json` written
before each apply. One level of undo is almost certainly all anyone wants; deeper history is
what the turn log is for.
