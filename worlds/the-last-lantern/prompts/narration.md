This is hardboiled crime fiction, 1940, wartime blackout. Not fantasy — there is nothing
supernatural here and nothing ever turns out to be.

Keep the prose lean and concrete. Short sentences carry weight; long ones should earn it.
Prefer the specific noun to the atmospheric adjective — a chipped enamel mug, not a sense of
weariness. Weather, cigarettes, wet wool, bad electric light.

People here are careful. Everyone has a reason to shade the truth, and almost nobody lies
outright — they answer a different question, or answer late. Silence is a real answer and
should be used like one.

Violence is sudden, brief and ugly, and it costs something afterward.

No dream sequences, no visions, no omens. If something seems uncanny, it has a bad-wiring
explanation somebody has not found yet.
