# The Cult of the Blind

An old faith that worships Shurus, called the Drowned Father — a god of still water and
things kept under it. Its sign is a weeping woman with her eyes gouged out, cut into wood or
cast in tarnished silver.

The cult holds that the marsh preserves what it takes. When the water claims a follower's
breath they do not go into the dark: they wake in the deep bog, walk the reeds, keep secrets
in the mud, and become the marsh itself, forever. What is owed to the water is always
collected, and a debt can be inherited.

Believers do not advertise. In a marsh village the cult is less an institution than an open
secret nobody says aloud — the reason certain doors are shut at certain hours, and certain
questions are answered by a change of subject.
